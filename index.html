Wake Up to the Past

Wake Up to the Past: Using Memory to Model Fluid Wake Effects on Robots

Luca Vendruscolo, Eduardo Sebastián, Amanda Prorok and Ajay Shankar. Preprint, March 2026, arXiv:2603.22472.

When one robot flies or swims through the wake another robot has left, the disturbance it feels depends on what the fluid was doing a moment ago. We measured this on a gantry: a tethered monocopter is moved through the downwash of another one while a time-of-flight sensor records its altitude.

Models that only see the current state get the disturbance wrong. Giving them a short history of past states, and having them estimate the transport delay, improves prediction substantially across quadrotor CFD, ship interaction, fish schooling, a 2D drone simulation and the real hardware.

Across the seven architectures we compared, models with memory cut RMSE by 35% on quadrotor CFD, 39% in closed-loop 2D drone tracking and 21% on the gantry hardware.

Reinforcement-learning marble labyrinth

2025. DreamerV3, YOLOv5, Arduino-driven steppers, 3D-printed re-loader.

An online reinforcement-learning system that learns to play a wooden marble labyrinth, the toy where two knobs tilt the board to steer the marble. Stepper motors attached to the knobs tilt the board, a camera with YOLOv5 tracks the marble, and DreamerV3 learns from experience.

A 3D-printed re-loader returns the marble after every attempt so training can run unsupervised. I changed the training loop so gradient updates only happen between episodes, which allows the controller to run at 45 Hz during live training.
