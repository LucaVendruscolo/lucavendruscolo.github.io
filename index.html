Wake Up to the Past

Wake Up to the Past: Using Memory to Model Fluid Wake Effects on Robots

Luca Vendruscolo, Eduardo Sebastián, Amanda Prorok and Ajay Shankar. Preprint, March 2026, arXiv:2603.22472.

My MPhil thesis was on predicting wake effects between robots. Drones and underwater robots move by pushing air or water, and the wake they leave, like the downwash under a drone, can push nearby robots off course. Solving the fluid dynamics in real time is far too slow, so these disturbances are usually learned from data.

Existing models only look at where the robots are right now, but a wake takes time to travel, so the push a robot feels now was caused by what the other robot was doing in the past. Giving the models a short memory of recent motion cut prediction error by 21% to 67% across drones, ships, a fish-inspired water tank and a monocopter gantry I built to test it on real hardware. Building simple physics into the model also helps when data is limited, matching the baseline with a fifth of the parameters.

Reinforcement-learning marble labyrinth

2024. DreamerV3, YOLOv5, Arduino-driven steppers, 3D-printed re-loader.

An online reinforcement-learning system that learns to play a wooden marble labyrinth, the toy where two knobs tilt the board to steer the marble. Stepper motors attached to the knobs tilt the board, a camera with YOLOv5 tracks the marble, and DreamerV3 learns from experience.

A 3D-printed re-loader returns the marble after every attempt so training can run unsupervised. I changed the training loop so gradient updates only happen between episodes, which allows the controller to run at 45 Hz during live training.

Three-board labyrinth workshop

A robotics and machine learning workshop where teams programmed three labyrinth boards to race a marble to the finish.

I was invited to run a workshop on robotics and machine learning at a summer school run by Li Peng. I turned the labyrinth into a game: each team programmed the moves for the boards, and the first team to get the marble to the end won a prize.

A Raspberry Pi hosted a website that the students connected to from their laptops. With three boards and a queue, every team could send an attempt, watch it run and change their code straight away.

Bike fitting from video

Saddle-height advice from a short phone video of someone cycling.

Professional bike fits are expensive, so with Kyle Fram and Fivos Papathanasiou I built a pipeline that takes a short video of someone cycling and says whether to raise or lower the saddle. It tracks the rider's joints and the bike, and a custom model estimates the bike's angle to the camera so the joint angles can be corrected for perspective.

To train that model we filmed riders with a Raspberry Pi and gyroscope on the bike recording its true angle. A 15 second clip takes about a minute to analyse, and you can try it with your own video at bikefitting.vercel.app.

Pill dispenser and reminder

A wall-mounted dispenser that releases one pill a day and keeps a calendar of every dose taken.

I kept forgetting to take my multivitamins and supplements, so I built a dispenser that stores the capsules and releases them one at a time.
